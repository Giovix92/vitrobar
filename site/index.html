Vitrobar

A glass taskbar for Windows 11.

Vitrobar replaces the stock taskbar with a frosted bar of its own: your apps, a Start panel, quick settings, a calendar and a few optional widgets. Free, open source, and it gives the Windows taskbar back the moment you quit.

Download for Windows Source on GitHub

x64 and ARM64. Early preview: the builds are not signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen asks before the first run.

What it does

The bar
Pinned and open apps with window dots, thumbnails on hover, tray icons with an overflow, and a clock. Pill, islands or full width; top or bottom; one bar per monitor.
Start
Its own Start panel: pinned apps, recent files, all apps, and search over apps, settings pages and files, with a calculator in the search box.
Quick settings
Volume and output device, brightness, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, battery and power mode, in a glass panel above the button.
Calendar
A month view on the clock, and a button to the Windows notification centre.
Widgets
Off until you turn them on: the music playing now, with play, pause and skip, and your Claude 5-hour and weekly limits, read from Claude Code's own status line.
Keyboard
The Windows key opens Vitrobar's Start; Win+1…9, Win+T and Win+B work on the bar. English and Italian.

Light on your PC

Idle, Vitrobar uses about 16 MB of memory and no CPU, with both widgets on. Nothing polls in the background: the bar wakes only when Windows tells it something changed.

It never changes Windows for good. A small watchdog brings the stock taskbar back at once if the bar crashes, and quitting Vitrobar leaves the taskbar exactly as it was.

Private by design

No account, no server, no telemetry. Vitrobar makes no network connections of its own. The Claude widget never reads your Claude login: it only reads what Claude Code already shows in its status line.

Install

  1. Download the zip for your PC from the latest release: x64 for most PCs, arm64 for Snapdragon laptops.
  2. Unzip it anywhere and run vitrobar.exe. The bar appears and the Windows taskbar steps aside.
  3. Right-click the bar for its settings. To stop, choose “Exit Vitrobar”: the Windows taskbar comes back.

Vitrobar starts with Windows by default; turn that off in its settings.
